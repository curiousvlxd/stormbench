# 0017. Model predictive control as a second center, played by the simulator

Status: accepted, 2026-10-17

## Context

EFD saves 6.4% of the overflow volume over the rain year. Sun et al. (2020) report 13.2% for model predictive control (MPC) on the same SWMM model ([Water 12(4), 1034](https://doi.org/10.3390/w12041034), Table 6). The testbed needed a second, stronger algorithm, a way to compare algorithms on equal terms, and a documented way for others to plug in their own. The architecture rule stays: the center proposes, the PLC stations check (ADR 0004, ADR 0007).

The paper's MPC (section 3):

- a conceptual internal model of linear reservoir tanks (passive and controlled outflow) and pipes as transport delays of 5 to 15 min (Table 2);
- overflow as an optimisation variable with a heavy cost;
- 5 min sampling time, prediction and control horizons of 100 min;
- "the assumption of perfect forecast, generated by precomputed simulations";
- a quadratic program solved with Mosek from MATLAB, coupled to SWMM through pyswmm.

The cost (eq. 14 to 16) weights the accumulated overflow volume per tank (Table 3: Tank 1 1,000, Tanks 2 to 5 5,000, Tank 6 10,000), the overflow flow (2), the flow to the treatment plant (-1) and the squared change of the control flow (0.01).

## Decision

- **Internal model, read from the `.inp` at run time** (`stormbench_sim/mpc.py`, `build_model`). Only the cost weights are taken from the paper.
  - Tanks in the model: the controlled ones and every tank downstream of one (T2, T3, T4, T6 and T1). T5 and the junction overflows CSO7 to CSO10 lie upstream of every controlled outlet, so control cannot change them; they enter as inflow.
  - Storage: mean surface area from the storage curve (the Astlingen curves are constant), full depth from `[STORAGE]`.
  - Outflow: SWMM's side orifice routine at full opening (orifice flow on the head above the opening's midpoint, weir flow while it is not submerged), with geometry and discharge coefficient from `[ORIFICES]` and `[XSECTIONS]`. In the LP the concave curve is bounded by its tangents over the predicted volume; the first step uses the exact value at the measured level. Checked against SWMM on the storm of 30 May with random settings every 5 min: the equation is within -0.1% to +0.3% (5th to 95th percentile) of SWMM's link flows on all five outlets.
  - Routing and delays: the network is walked downstream from each outlet to the next tank; conduit travel times at full-flow Manning velocity, rounded to 5 min steps. Every route takes 2.5 to 3.7 min, so every delay is one step (the paper's conceptual model uses 5 to 15 min).
- **Forecast: perfect, from a pre-run.** Before the run, a Base Case run of the same window records every minute the flow of each link that brings uncontrollable water into a modelled tank's route (C18, C17, C7, C10, C20, C22 and C1, the outflow of T5) and the lateral inflow at the route's nodes, averaged per 5 min interval and shifted by its delay. This matches the paper's setting (a perfect forecast from precomputed simulations) and is an upper bound for what a real forecast allows. Against an EFD run of 4 October, the forecast inflow volumes of T2, T4 and T6 agree to within 1 m³. A second variant, `--mpc-forecast persistence`, holds the inflow just measured for the whole horizon, to show how much of the gain is foresight.
- **Linear program per step.**
  - Variables per horizon step k and modelled tank i: outflow q, overflow w (nonnegative slack) and volume V; per controlled outlet, the absolute change of its flow.
  - Constraints: the volume balance with the upstream outflows of k minus the delay (earlier steps from the recorded history), 0 <= V <= full volume, 0 <= q <= outflow at full opening.
  - Cost: the paper's eq. 14 with its weights, including the accumulated overflow volume, except that the change of the control flow costs its absolute value instead of its square, which keeps the problem linear.
  - Solved with HiGHS through `scipy.optimize.linprog`. The matrices are built once; each step changes only right-hand sides and bounds.
  - Treatment plant capacity needs no extra constraint: the plant receives what T1's uncontrolled outlet V1 passes, which the model limits by the orifice equation.
- **Setting from flow.** The planned outflow of the first step is converted into a gate setting by inverting the orifice equation at the measured level (bisection), clipped to 0..1. The other planned steps are discarded (receding horizon).
- **Where it runs: the simulator plays the center.** An LP in .NET would need a native solver (OR-Tools or a HiGHS wrapper), the forecast published on the UNS, and parity tests against the Python reference; that did not fit the time available. Instead, `PlcController` takes any `runner.Center` and writes its proposal into the PLC's center registers with the step, as the existing emulated center does for tests.
  - Labelled where a user sees it: the console algorithm is "MPC, simulator as center", its catalog entry says `center: simulator`, and Scada's EFD responder stays silent while the console runs an algorithm of another center, so the PLC receives exactly one proposal per step.
  - The PLC runs in mode 3 and validates every proposal as before; the ST program is unchanged.
  - The shadow check stays exact because the reference recomputes the stations' decision from exactly the proposal written (64-bit LREAL registers, no rounding).
- **Plugin point.** `runner.Center` (propose, observe) and `--center package.module:factory` let anyone run their own center offline or with the PLC; see [docs/controllers.md](../controllers.md). `python -m stormbench_sim compare` puts Base Case, EFD and MPC side by side.

## Results

Python only, no PLC, proposals checked by the Python reference of the stations (`python -m stormbench_sim compare --year`, [`results/controllers.md`](../../results/controllers.md)). Overflow volume in m³, each window started with empty tanks:

| Storm | Base Case | EFD | MPC | MPC, no forecast |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 May 2000, 36 hours | 12,931 | 11,967 (-7.5%) | 10,419 (-19.4%) | 10,323 (-20.2%) |
| 29 May to 1 June 2000 | 13,476 | 12,070 (-10.4%) | 10,533 (-21.8%) | 10,576 (-21.5%) |
| 4 October 2000 | 28,345 | 28,260 (-0.3%) | 27,760 (-2.1%) | 27,714 (-2.2%) |
| 19 August 2000 | 25,659 | 25,800 (+0.5%) | 24,036 (-6.3%) | 24,157 (-5.9%) |
| Year 2000, SWMM report totals | 264,724 | 247,787 (-6.40%) | 201,934 (-23.72%) | 214,716 (-18.89%) |
| Year 2000, paper (Tables 4 to 6) | 264,731 | 247,788 (-6.40%) | 229,750 (-13.21%) | |

- Solve time per step over the year: 7.5 ms mean, 13.3 ms at the 99th percentile, 74 ms maximum, no failed solve in 105,406 steps (16 runs in parallel on a 16-thread desktop). The MPC year takes 844 s of wall time plus 27 s for the forecast pre-run.
- Water is conserved: over the year the MPC spills 62,790 m³ less than the Base Case and sends 63,924 m³ more to the treatment plant.
- The MPC beats EFD in every window, including the two largest storms, where EFD gains nothing.

## Consequences

- **An upper bound, stated as such.** The forecast is perfect and comes from the same model the MPC controls; real rain forecasts are uncertain. The persistence variant shows that most of the gain over EFD does not come from foresight: in the four storms both variants are within 1%, and over the year foresight is worth 4.8 percentage points (mostly at T1). The gain comes from using the outlet capacity of all tanks together while keeping T1 below its top.
- **Better than the paper's MPC; the reason is not verified.** Our internal model is derived from the SWMM model it controls (outlet equation, measured inflows at the tank inlets); the paper's comes from the conceptual benchmark description with linear reservoirs and longer delays. A smaller model mismatch is the likely reason. The paper's MPC spills 23% more than its Base Case at T3, ours 9% less. Not verified against the paper's code.
- **Overflow is a slack.** The LP may plan a spill at a tank that is not full when that pays; the weights make upstream spills more expensive, and SWMM decides what actually spills. Planned and real overflow can differ.
- **Model scope.** Tree networks, rectangular side orifices, tabular storage curves; anything else raises `NotImplementedError` instead of producing a wrong model.
- **Center link in the console.** The 2 s center watchdog (ADR 0011) does not apply to a center played by the simulator, because the proposal travels with the step; cutting the link from the console works as for EFD. The Live view still draws the link from the PLC to the control room. A Stop during the forecast pre-run takes effect after it.
- **CLI runs against a shared PLC.** Outside the console, Scada cannot tell that the simulator plays the center. `--center mpc` (and the emulated EFD) need a PLC without the UNS stack, as in `docker compose up -d plc`.
- **Not yet measured with the PLC in the loop.** The fake-link tests and the Python twin cover the path; `PLC_HOST=localhost PLC_PORT=1502 uv run pytest -m plc -k mpc` runs the three-day storm through the PLC and requires every answer to equal the reference.
